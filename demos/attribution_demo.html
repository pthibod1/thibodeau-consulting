Attribution

Why last-click lies, and how to measure what your ads actually caused

Most attribution credits whoever was clicked last. But the channel clicked last is often just standing where demand was already heading. This walks through the trap, the hidden cause, and the experiment that fixes it. The data comes from a known "true" model, so you can see exactly how far last-click drifts from the truth.

1Last-click looks decisive, until you change the rule

Rule-based attribution divides credit among the touchpoints on the path to a sale. Switch the rule and the "winning" channel changes, even though nothing about the business changed. That's the tell: these models reshuffle credit, they don't measure cause.

2The hidden cause, demand drives both clicks and sales

People who already want you search your brand name and click the ad on the way to buying. So demand causes the branded-search click and the sale, the click didn't cause much of anything. Below, the same conversions split into what's truly ad-driven vs. organic demand that would have converted anyway. Slide how much branded search rides on demand and watch its last-click credit balloon while its real contribution barely moves.

3The experiment, turn it off and watch what happens

Causation needs a counterfactual: what would sales have been without the channel? A geo holdout turns the channel off in a random set of markets (control) and leaves it on elsewhere (treatment). The gap between them is the true incremental lift, with an honest confidence interval. Pick a channel and a holdout size.

4The verdict, last-click would defund what's working

Put the two side by side: return on ad spend as last-click believes it, vs. return measured by experiment. Branded search looks like a star on last-click because it's billing you for organic demand. The channel that actually creates customers is the one last-click would cut.
